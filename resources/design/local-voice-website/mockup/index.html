How the pieces cooperate

A playful picture.
A real process.

Inference

How does an answer form?

The transcript and a brief system instruction become the language model’s input. It generates text a token at a time, using patterns learned during training. A token may be a word or part of one; the little tiles above are a simplified illustration. There is no web lookup, and an answer can still be wrong.

Compute

Three models. One device.

Whisper tiny.en recognizes speech. Gemma 4 E2B generates a text answer. Piper’s LJ Speech medium voice model synthesizes the reply. The selected profile uses whisper.cpp and llama.cpp with CUDA for recognition and answering, and Piper on CPU for speech. The models load before the device becomes ready and stay in memory. Complete phrases pass to Piper as Gemma generates; playback can overlap later generation and synthesis. The stages above separate the jobs for clarity; they are software sharing one Jetson, not separate physical modules.

Boundaries

A fresh start, each time.

The current device handles English, one question at a time. There is no conversation history, web search, document retrieval, or cloud fallback. Temporary audio is cleaned up on normal completion, cancellation, and ordinary errors.

Under the paper: models, runtime & privacy +

This illustration follows the current Gemma/Piper deployment: Whisper tiny.en F16, Gemma 4 E2B instruction-tuned Q4_K_M, and Piper 1.8.0 with en_US-ljspeech-medium (22,050 Hz audio). The Gemma path receives text from Whisper; it does not use Gemma’s native audio capability. Thinking output is disabled. Piper’s embedded eSpeak phonemizer supplies pronunciation units; its neural ONNX voice model produces the waveform. The illustration is conceptual, not a literal layer count, measured spectrogram, exact tokenizer trace, or board layout. The enclosure remains a form concept.

Technology references: Whisper, Google Gemma, and Piper neural speech.

The answer context is 2,048 tokens, with a default 128-token response cap. Device mode speaks complete phrases as they arrive. If the output limit or a later stream error stops generation, remaining speech stops; phrases already spoken cannot be retracted. The command-line path validates the complete answer before export. Runtime commits, model revisions, and SHA-256 hashes are pinned in the runtime lock.

Initial setup downloads software and weights. The deployed service and its three model/voice child processes have only loopback in their network namespaces; this is a boundary around the service, not a claim that the entire board has no network. Temporary audio uses RAM-backed storage. Abrupt process kills and operating-system behavior have separate retention limits; no forensic-erasure guarantee is made.

Build log · November 15, 2026

Built. Running.
Still learning.

The physical device has moved beyond parts on a list. Here is what the current evidence establishes—and what is still being tested.

Hardware and software status

A working device. An evolving stack.

The physical Jetson has completed standalone button-to-spoken-answer checks, including use with the laptop disconnected. The headless service and startup after reboot have also been exercised.

Whisper, Gemma 4 E2B, and Piper now stay loaded, with answer generation, phrase synthesis, and playback overlapping. All 55 regression checks passed. Two confirmed live answers reached the playback request in 1.113 and 1.061 seconds after capture ended; acoustic onset was not instrumented. Nine short authored WAV trials reached playback requests in 0.795–0.970 seconds (median 0.827), excluding microphone capture and acoustic onset. These small samples do not establish a universal sub-second response. New profile-specific reboot/disconnection checks and long-session qualification remain open. Read the deployment record.

A work in progress

From an idea
to something you can hold.

Local Voice is a local speech-and-answer project from Long Tail Inference Lab. The hardware story above preserves the enclosure concept while the working prototype develops on an open, mains-powered board.

The first build uses a Jetson Orin Nano Super, a USB microphone and speaker, a physical button, and mains power. The standalone hardware loop has been demonstrated with the laptop disconnected. The current resident Gemma/Piper deployment has also completed two confirmed live spoken answers, with approximately one-second playback-request delays. Broader voice quality and reliability checks remain open.

The illustrations simplify the components. The final enclosure and cable routing are conceptual; a screen, battery, and portable enclosure are deferred. The first-year ceiling is $1,000, including required new hardware, software, distribution fees, tax, and shipping. Scored quality, sustained reliability, and cross-platform evaluation remain open.
